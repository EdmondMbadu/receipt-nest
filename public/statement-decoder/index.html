The problem

A statement says where you paid. It almost never says what for.

SQ *GRNLINE PLMB SUPWhy it looks like this

Small businesses take cards through payment companies, so SQ * (Square) comes first, and names are cut to about 22 characters.

SHOPIFY* 104729381Why it looks like this

Some charges are bills behind a number. This one is a store’s monthly plan, not a purchase from a shop.

PURCHASE AUTHORIZED ON 10/04Why it looks like this

Banks add their own words, so the store name gets pushed off the end of the line.

At tax time, every one of those turns into the same question: “What was this for?”

Honest by design

What it won’t pretend to know.

  • A statement never lists items. The decoder tells you who charged you and the likely category. It won’t invent what you bought.
  • Uncertain lines are flagged. If a code is ambiguous, you’ll see “check this” instead of a confident wrong answer.
  • “No receipt” means none saved in ReceiptNest. It can’t see the paper in your drawer. It shows you which charges to look for, and the receipt is what turns a guess into a record.
  • Not tax advice. Categories are suggestions. You and your bookkeeper make the call.
Your statement, handled with care

Questions

The short answers.

Does it connect to my bank?

No. You upload a statement file you already have, a PDF or CSV. ReceiptNest never asks for your bank login.

Can it tell me exactly what I bought?

No tool can, from a statement alone. It tells you who charged you and the likely category, and marks lines it isn’t sure about. Your receipts are what show the items, and the decoder shows which charges don’t have one saved yet.

How does it know which charges need a receipt?

It only knows about receipts you’ve saved in ReceiptNest, whether you snapped them or forwarded them. Each statement charge is matched to those receipts by amount, date and merchant, with room for things like a tip added after you signed, or a charge that posts a few days late. If nothing matches, the charge is listed as needing a receipt. If a charge could match more than one receipt, it’s marked “check this” so you can confirm.

Which banks will it work with?

The goal is any bank that lets you download a statement as a PDF or CSV. CSV statements come first because every bank lays out its PDFs differently.

When will it be ready?

We’re building it now. Join the list and you’ll get one email the day it opens, with your first statement free.

Is this tax or accounting advice?

No. Categories are suggestions to save you and your bookkeeper time. Your bookkeeper or tax professional makes the final call.

What should I do in the meantime?

Start keeping your receipts in ReceiptNest. When the decoder opens, it matches statement lines to the receipts you already have. If a charge leaves you puzzled today, our free guide to bank statement codes explains the common ones.
