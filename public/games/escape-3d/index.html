The last 100 days before tax day

In the game, the camera pushes in when 100 days are left. In real life, the last 100 days start around New Year’s.

From January 1 to mid-April is about 105 days. For most individual US federal returns, that’s the whole gap between the end of the tax year and the filing deadline. It’s also when a year of receipts either comes together in an evening or turns into a weekend in a shoebox.

Here’s a countdown that keeps the receipt side of it calm. Dates below are the usual ones; they move when they fall on a weekend or holiday.

A receipt countdown to tax day

  1. 100DAYS
    Early January: close the year. Check that every month from January to December has its receipts, and download your December bank and card statements. If you make quarterly estimated tax payments, the last one for the year is generally due in mid-January.
  2. 75DAYS
    End of January: collect the forms. Employers generally have to send Form W-2 by January 31, and businesses that paid you as a contractor generally have to send Form 1099-NEC by the same date. File them with the year’s receipts.
  3. 60DAYS
    February: match and fill the gaps. Go month by month against your statements. For each charge with no receipt, search your email, check the seller’s account page or ask for a copy. A statement line shows that you paid, not what you bought: here’s what a bank statement can and can’t replace. If you’re starting from a pile, sort the shoebox first.
  4. 30DAYS
    Mid-March: hand it over. Give whoever prepares your return the whole year in one place, sorted by month, with a short list of the charges you still can’t place. Here’s how to organize receipts for your accountant.
  5. 0DAYS
    Tax day. Most individual federal returns are due in mid-April. If you need more time, you can request an extension to file, but the IRS is clear that an extension to file is not an extension to pay.

After filing, keep the records. The IRS generally expects them to be kept for three years after you file, longer in some situations. Here’s how long to keep receipts, rule by rule.

This is general record-keeping information, not tax advice. Deadlines can change, so check IRS.gov for the current year, and ask your tax professional what your situation needs.

Why days break in bunches

Every sphere has one round hole, and all the holes turn around the same tilted axis. Each sphere runs a little behind the one inside it, so most of the time the holes are scattered and the receipt just bounces. Every so often they line up into a tunnel, and the receipt drops through five or ten days in a second. That’s the glass rain.

Nobody can plan a run: the receipt’s speed, the tilt of the axis and where the holes start change every time. In 600 test runs, about 4 in 10 escaped before tax day, and the typical escape came with around ten seconds to spare.

Receipts work the same way. Snap them as they come and the months line up on their own. Leave them, and April is 365 spheres deep.

How to play Escape the Year in 3D

  • A receipt bounces inside 365 nested glass spheres, one for every day of the year. Each sphere has a round hole, and the holes slowly turn.
  • When the receipt slips through a hole, that sphere shatters and the days-left counter drops.
  • The tax-day clock gives it 50 seconds. Break all 365 and the receipt escapes.
  • Watch lets it run on its own. Help it gives you three nudges: tap anywhere and the receipt is pushed toward your finger.
  • Drag at any time to turn the spheres and look from another side.
  • Every bounce plays the next note of In the Hall of the Mountain King.

Prefer it flat? The original Escape the Year uses 365 rings instead of spheres.

Questions

Is this the same game as Escape the Year?

Same idea, new shape. The rings became glass spheres, and the receipt can now fly in any direction instead of around a flat circle. The physics were retuned so the odds stay close: about 4 in 10 runs escape.

Can the receipt always escape?

No. Plenty of runs end with tax day winning and a couple of hundred days still trapped. Your three nudges help most when you time them for a moment when the hole is turning toward the receipt.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes, in any modern browser with 3D graphics (WebGL) turned on. On slower phones it lowers its resolution to keep moving smoothly. If 3D isn’t available, the 2D version runs everywhere.

When is tax day?

For most individual US federal returns, the due date is in mid-April, and it moves when it falls on a weekend or holiday. Check IRS.gov for the current year’s date.
