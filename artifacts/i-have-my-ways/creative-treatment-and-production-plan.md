# I HAVE MY WAYS

ReceiptNest animated short — creative treatment and production plan, version 2: Firefly production route.

Prepared October 2, 2026; production status updated October 3, 2026. Character artwork, scene artwork, a Firefly animation pilot, original music, and a 60-second visual workprint have now been created. The complete character-animation film is unfinished. See [production status](production/PRODUCTION-STATUS.md) for verified deliverables, limitations, and the exact continuation point. The production plan below describes the intended final film, not a claim that all stages have been completed.

Production decision: use Adobe Firefly as the primary generation workspace and Photoshop, Premiere, and optionally After Effects for preparation and finishing. No Higgsfield credits are required. Preserve the revised story and visual direction. The model, credit budget, and exact dialogue-sync method will be selected from the owner's available Firefly tools and tested in the pilot.

## Creative premise

Nia and Marcus build beautiful things together. Running their small furniture and home-goods business has gradually taken over the place where they used to have dinner. One small receipt habit gives them that space, and their evenings, back.

Keep the title and the mirrored “I have my ways” exchange. Give the comedy an affectionate relationship underneath it: Marcus quietly follows through on their promise to improve things; Nia recognizes that effort, adopts the habit, and eventually takes care of the handoff herself.

The emotional progression is exhaustion → curiosity → surprise → practical relief → shared joke → reclaimed time. Each product moment changes something for the couple: capture builds the habit, the monthly view answers a question, retrieval saves a frustrating search, export closes the loop.

### What changes from the original

- Give their business a visible identity: furniture sketches, wood samples, a half-built stool, delivery tags, and a workshop apron. This connects the supply store, lumber, drill, and client coffee.
- Establish an interrupted dinner in the opening, then repeat the exact framing with dinner restored at the end.
- Show the drill purchase in the supply-store scene so Marcus finding that receipt later pays off a specific earlier action.
- Nia's hesitation comes from a familiar paper habit. Marcus's improvement is quiet follow-through. Both are capable business owners.
- Let numbers and category details live mainly on screen. Dialogue gets room for character reactions.
- Introduce the ReceiptNest name on the capture screen, then reinforce it during retrieval and the end card.
- Replace inconsistent calendar dates with relative time labels. A specific year is unnecessary.
- Show export and a separate email handoff, without implying a built-in accountant messaging service.
- End with “Start free.” The current web product places PDF exports in Pro.

## Character direction

### Nia

Adult woman, approximately mid-thirties. Coral knit sweater, natural textured hair, gold hoops, practical dark trousers. Proposed casting/design direction: a Black couple, subject to the owner's creative preference. Their identity and appearance stay fixed across every shot.

She leads design and finishing in their business: observant, practical, good with her hands, and quick with a dry joke. The opening fatigue is about the situation they share. Let affection remain visible when she challenges Marcus.

Signature acting: one eyebrow rises before she speaks; she checks an object, then looks at Marcus; the smallest delayed smile reveals that she appreciates him. Her adoption happens first in her eyes, then in her hand reaching for her own phone.

Voice direction: warm mid-range voice, conversational English, precise but unhurried. Avoid a scolding delivery. The final “I have my ways” should echo Marcus's rhythm while adding her own quiet satisfaction. Audition accent and vocal character before committing; do not infer an accent from appearance.

### Marcus

Adult man, approximately late-thirties. Muted green hoodie, short beard, relaxed silhouette, practical shoes. He handles building, deliveries, and much of the purchasing. A pencil behind his ear belongs in workshop shots, not every setting.

He is resourceful and slightly mischievous. His problem in the opening is remembering old purchases after a long year. He takes their agreement seriously and experiments with a habit the next morning.

Signature acting: an open-palmed shrug, a small glance to check Nia's response, one asymmetric smile. In the drill scene, he offers help immediately. Save his theatrical pride for “I have my ways.”

Voice direction: warm lower register, understated humor, natural breaths, a little hesitation in the opening. Keep the exact monthly total matter-of-fact; he is reading his records, not performing a mathematical trick.

### Their relationship

Use small gestures to establish familiarity: Marcus makes room for Nia's mug; Nia nudges a tool back within his reach; their final shoulder contact feels unconscious. Choose one or two gestures that fit the animatic rather than adding all of them.

## Revised 60-second shooting script

All timings below are editorial targets. Recorded dialogue and the animatic must demonstrate that the pauses fit before detailed animation begins. Dollar amounts are written as display values; record their full, natural spoken forms.

### 00:00–00:08 — The dinner they missed

KITCHEN, NIGHT. Clock: 11:02. Cool window light, one warm lamp. An overhead shot reveals receipts spread across their dining table. Two forgotten takeout boxes have been pushed to the edges. A furniture sketch is trapped underneath the paper.

Nia raises a crumpled receipt. Insert: **MISC · $86.00**. Marcus looks from the receipt to his cold dinner.

**NIA:** “Marcus. What's ‘miscellaneous, eighty-six dollars’?”

**MARCUS:** “…It made sense in March.”

Nia exhales. Their eyes meet: shared exhaustion, a tiny smile.

**NIA:** “We're fixing this.”

Marcus nods. One receipt slips over the edge of his takeout box.

Sound: clock tick, dry paper rustle, one quiet comic musical punctuation after Marcus's answer. Keep the opening intimate.

### 00:08–00:14 — A small change

SUPPLY STORE, DAY. Label: **NEXT MORNING**.

A boxed 18V drill kit slides across the counter. The cashier hands over its receipt. Marcus flattens it and photographs it through ReceiptNest. Make the app name readable once; do not interrupt the action with a separate logo presentation.

Nia holds a canvas tote, notices the camera gesture, and raises one eyebrow.

**NIA:** “You're photographing receipts now?”

**MARCUS:** “Trying something.”

He pockets his phone. She tucks the paper receipt into the tote's side pocket. This is the same receipt that returns in the garage scene.

Receipt continuity: **Tool Depot · 18V Drill Kit · $129.00**. Preserve the retailer, amount, item wording, paper layout, and drill design later.

Sound: counter slide, shutter, soft confirmation after a visible processing transition. The shutter introduces the rhythm of the music.

### 00:14–00:18 — A habit, in three beats

Three quick cuts, each with a different composition. Keep the character's hands, phone, and sleeve consistent.

1. Gas station: receipt beside the fuel pump; camera shutter; **Gas · $52.10**.
2. Lumber yard: wood stacked in the background; camera shutter; **Supplies · $214.60**.
3. Client coffee: two cups and a furniture sketch; camera shutter; **Client meals · $18.40**.

App category fields can remain visible as secondary detail. These fast inserts do not require the audience to read every label. A match cut from capture to processed receipt compresses elapsed time; it does not promise instant processing.

Music grows from the shutter rhythm into a light instrumental groove. No dialogue.

### 00:18–00:28 — The first “ways”

LIVING ROOM, EVENING. Label: **MONTH END**.

Nia settles beside Marcus with a mug and their sketchbook. She glances at a page of plans.

**NIA:** “This month's damage?”

Marcus checks his phone.

**MARCUS:** “Two thousand, nine hundred sixty-one dollars. Forty cents.”

His screen is the evidence: **This month · $2,961.40**, with a compact receipt spending breakdown. Proposed demo figures:

| Category | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Supplies | $1,240.00 |
| Gas & Fuel | $612.00 |
| Client meals | $188.00 |
| Other | $921.40 |
| Total | $2,961.40 |

Use actual product styling and supported labels after the native-app demo is checked. The total represents saved receipts. Do not introduce a bank balance or bank-sync animation.

Nia studies him.

**NIA:** “…How?”

Marcus looks up, lifts one eyebrow, and allows himself a small smile.

**MARCUS:** “I have my ways.”

Hold a short reaction beat. The music answers with a small three-note phrase that will return with Nia.

### 00:28–00:38 — The receipt that matters

GARAGE WORKSHOP, DAY. Label: **SATURDAY**.

Nia is assembling a stool. The same drill sputters and stops. A small puff, then silence. She sets it down and searches her tote. A parking stub, gum wrapper, and folded paper emerge. The drill receipt is badly faded.

**NIA:** “Where's the receipt?”

Marcus is at the adjacent bench. He has recognized the purchase and found the saved original.

**MARCUS:** “This one?”

He offers his phone. Insert: the clear stored receipt image with **Tool Depot · 18V Drill Kit · $129.00**.

Nia looks at the faded paper, then the readable original on screen, then him. One slow blink. Her shoulders release. A small appreciative smile.

Her own phone rises into frame. Marcus notices. He lets her keep the moment.

Sound: sputter, brief silence, tote rustles, restrained musical warmth. Allow roughly a second of silent acting after recognition. The scene proves retrieval; it does not depict or guarantee a successful warranty claim.

### 00:38–00:46 — The second “ways”

KITCHEN, MORNING. Label: **A WEEK LATER**.

The table has room for two cups and their sketchbook. Nia has ReceiptNest open. Marcus leans in with a teasing challenge.

**MARCUS:** “Coffee this month?”

She checks the saved coffee records without making a production of it.

**NIA:** “Forty-six twenty.”

Insert or restrained editorial overlay: **Coffee · this month · $46.20**. Use the real merchant/category/filter view once verified; any overlay is visibly part of the film's graphics, not an invented app feature. This is a later month than the $18.40 client-coffee montage, so no unexplained subtotal conflict is created.

Marcus pauses, delighted.

**MARCUS:** “…How?”

Nia raises the same eyebrow he raised earlier.

**NIA:** “I have my ways.”

She takes a satisfied sip; he breaks into a smile. Repeat the same camera distance and three-note music phrase as his earlier line.

### 00:46–00:56 — Their evening, returned

KITCHEN, NIGHT. Label: **NEXT TAX SEASON**.

Repeat the opening overhead composition. Clock: 11:02. Same table, same lamp. Two open takeout boxes now occupy the places where receipts were piled. A completed furniture piece is subtly visible beyond the table.

Marcus reaches for his food, then catches himself.

**MARCUS:** “Receipts?”

Nia briefly shows her phone.

**NIA:** “Already with our accountant.”

A brief insert shows an ordinary sent email with **Business receipts.pdf** attached. A small “PDF export · Pro” treatment accompanies the product demonstration. The email screen is separate from ReceiptNest; no real email is sent during production. Avoid the original Jan–Sep date range and unsupported in-app “Sent to accountant” badge.

**MARCUS:** “When did we even do them?”

**NIA:** “Every day.”

She puts her phone face down and nudges his takeout box toward him. He relaxes, shoulder touching hers. They finally eat. End on the couple's shared moment.

Music resolves warmly; clock ticking falls beneath the room tone. The final gesture gets a clear beat before the brand appears.

### 00:56–01:00 — End card

Warm off-white background. The existing ReceiptNest mark settles gently into place. Keep all copy visible early in the four-second hold.

**ReceiptNest**

**Just snap.**

Start free · iOS & Android

**receipt-nest.com**

Small, readable supporting line: **CSV export included · PDF with Pro**.

Optional spoken brand line: “ReceiptNest. Just snap.” Use one of the established voices. Test whether the quiet musical finish is stronger before committing to that extra line.

## Visual design system

Textured illustrated 2D characters in layered environments, with depth from perspective, lighting, cast shadows, and restrained camera moves. Adult proportions, expressive hands, rounded but specific facial features, natural hair with a clearly designed silhouette, tactile clothes, and warm skin shading.

This plan produces a polished illustrated short. A fully modeled and rigged 3D film would be a separate pipeline and should be scoped before building these assets.

### Assets to build

Two character model sheets with front, three-quarter, and profile views; height comparison; stable hair and beard shapes; outfit callouts; hand poses; expression range; and phone/receipt grip poses. These serve as references for deliberately composed shot frames. Separate mouth shapes and fully layered puppets are needed only if a shot uses the optional manual-animation route.

Expression set for each: neutral, listening, tired, doubtful, sheepish, curious, surprised, pleased, amused, and relieved. Draw purposeful extremes before interpolating between them.

Four hero environments: kitchen, store counter, living room, garage. Kitchen needs matched day and night versions. Three compact montage backgrounds: gas, lumber, coffee. Reuse the same camera geometry for opening/end and the same close-up framing for both catchphrases.

Hero props: the drill and its box; one consistently designed drill receipt; the crumpled MISC receipt; tote; two identifiable mugs; two phones with consistent cases; clock; lamp; takeout boxes; furniture sketch; stool and completed furniture detail.

Phone screens and monetary text are separate editable graphics using real app references. Do not bake important words, numbers, or the brand mark into generated character artwork.

### Animation quality rules

- Plan thought before speech: eye movement → slight head/body response → line.
- Use deliberate held poses for jokes and recognition. Continuous idle movement would weaken these moments.
- Inspect lip sync against the exact selected dialogue. Reference-guided video is not a guarantee of accurate speech, stable voices, or correct speaker assignment. Regenerate or use a controlled animation pass when necessary.
- Keep feet, mugs, phones, and fingertips in believable contact with surfaces.
- Add subtle overlap to hair, sleeves, and earrings only where the body's motion calls for it.
- Guide head turns with consistent designed views and inspect the face throughout each shot.
- Prepare specific key poses for drill handling, searching the tote, phone handoff, and sipping. Keep each generated shot focused on one action, and check hand/object contact throughout.
- Keep screen inserts readable at normal viewing size and check a phone-sized preview.
- Protect the four-second end card and the silent beat after the drill-receipt reveal when tightening timing.

## Sound and music plan

Dialogue is the foundation. Prefer Firefly Generate Speech, using either Firefly Speech or a speech model available inside the owner's Adobe account. Keep each character's selected voice and settings consistent. Save separate dialogue files, take names, and transcripts; normalize production audio to 48 kHz WAV after checking the downloaded format. Generate a small set of contrasting performance takes; select by acting and measured duration. A standalone ElevenLabs account is not a baseline dependency.

For visible dialogue, test an audio-capable video model inside Firefly with the approved character references and, where supported, the selected voice take as an audio reference. Adobe documents such reference and synchronized-audio controls for Seedance 2.0; availability and quality on this account remain unverified. An audio reference guides generation and does not guarantee verbatim speech or an identical voice. Do not simply place a different voice take over already speaking generated mouths and call it lip-synced.

No narrator is required. Keep the two voices consistent through all scenes. Use licensed stock voices or newly designed fictional voices; neither character requires cloning a person.

Music direction: light instrumental groove around 90–100 BPM, with soft keys, plucked bass, and restrained brushed or hand percussion. Leave the opening relatively bare; introduce rhythm with the receipt habit. A short three-note phrase accompanies each “I have my ways,” then resolves at dinner. Avoid vocals that compete with dialogue.

Foley: distinct paper textures, tote fabric, counter slide, ceramic mug contact, coffee sip, phone shutter, subtle app confirmation, drill sputter, and takeout-box movement. Environment beds should be quiet and specific to each location.

Mix dialogue, music, and effects on separate tracks. Start with a web master near -16 LUFS integrated and no peaks above -1 dBTP, adjusting for the eventual delivery platform. Check intelligibility on headphones, laptop speakers, phone speakers, and mono playback. Keep a clean dialogue stem, music stem, and effects stem.

Prefer Firefly Generate Music for the score and Generate Sound Effects for individual foley cues, subject to the account's available features. Generate to the edited film's length and adjust the music in Premiere around dialogue and reaction beats. Keep separate downloads where supported. Adobe documents these features; this account's generation entitlements and any selected model's usage terms still need checking. A licensed existing track remains an alternative if the generated score does not suit the film.

### Credit planning

Estimate against generated duration, including retries and unused clip tails, rather than the finished film's duration. Budget = each submitted clip's displayed cost + image/voice/music/effects generation + a stated retry allowance. Set a pilot ceiling before committing to the full film.

Partner model rates and account offers differ. This is a published-rate example, not a quote for this film or a verified rate on the owner's account.
